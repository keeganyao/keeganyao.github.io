I am a recent Ph.D. graduate in computer science from Duke University, advised by <a href="https://users.cs.duke.edu/~pankaj/">Pankaj K. Agarwal</a>. My research focuses on designing simple and scalable algorithms for the _optimal transport (OT) problem_, a common method of lifting geometry of points to geometry of distributions across mathematics, statistics, data science and machine learning. The algorithms I design combine the orthogonal strengths of numerical and combinatorial optimization methods: numerical methods enjoy simple and elegant certificates of progress, while combinatorial algorithms enjoy simple iterative steps. My studies since graduation have focused on various extensions of OT to applications in data science and machine learning with the aim to highlight the orthogonal strengths of numerical and combinatorial methods when applied to them. Applications include but are not limited to: clustering of distributions, dynamic OT under distributional drift, symmetry-preserving transport plans, flow matchings, and rewiring for graph neural networks.

Before attending Duke University, I completed my undergraduate studies in mathematics and computer science at the University of Connecticut. While there, I had the great privilege of exploring many diverse fields across mathematics and computer science. My undergraduate honors thesis, under the supervision of <a href="https://www2.math.uconn.edu/~munteanu/">Ovidiu Munteanu</a>, was an expository paper on the existence of the Ricci flow under negative curvature lower bounds. In addition, I studied geometric data analysis of trajectories under <a href="https://donsheehy.net/">Don Sheehy</a>, comparison of phylogenetic trees with partially overlapping taxa under <a href="https://mukul-bansal.uconn.edu/">Mukul Bansal</a>, and high dimensional entropic uncertainty relations with applications to quantum cryptography under <a href="https://walterkrawec.org/">Walter Krawec</a>.
